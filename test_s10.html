Instead of fitting clearly into one category, Mannequin Bass seems to exist between body, object, sculpture, and the visual language of a musical instrument.

This ambiguity is important because each category changes the way I understand the work. When I see it as a sculpture, I pay attention to its form and materials. When I notice its resemblance to an instrument, I think about how strongly its form suggests sound even though the work itself remains silent. When I see the mannequin as a body, the instrument-like elements begin to feel almost like an extension of that body.

The work becomes interesting precisely because it cannot be understood through only one of these categories. Its ambiguity keeps shifting the boundary between body and object, and between sculpture and instrument‑like form.
